Ballot questions

Miami Beach Referendum: 1691 Michigan Avenue lease (Rivani)

City of Miami Beach only. Special election on the same day.

🪨 Rivani man rent city cave-garage. YES: man get rent cave 40 more winter, build big office on top of garage, pay city more shiny rock (3% not 2.5%). NO: old deal stay. Nothing new build. Old lease still alive.

YES means: The city amends its ground lease with RK Rivani LLC for the city-owned garage lot at 1691 Michigan Ave: two extra 20-year options (lease could run 106 more years), about 47,000 sq ft of new office space above the garage (up to 100 ft), percentage rent rises from 2.5% to 3%, and the tenant provides $1.5M in public benefits.
NO means: The lease amendment does not happen. The existing lease and its current terms stay in place (it is not cancelled).

Supporters cited by news coverage include Mayor Steven Meiner, Commissioner Joe Magazine and a Lincoln Road PAC (more revenue, higher rent, public benefits). Opponents include Commissioner David Suarez and both the Miami Beach Democratic and Republican clubs (long private control of public land, no competitive bidding, reduced parking). Ballot dollar figures: $247.7M guaranteed base rent and ~$184.1M estimated percentage rent over the 40 option years.

Sources: Miami-Dade Elections, Miami Beach Special Election Sample Ballot, Nov 3, 2026; City of Miami Beach Clerk, Nov 3, 2026 Special Election page & Related Resolutions (accessed Oct 26, 2026); Miami Herald, Should Miami Beach extend the Rivani lease by 40 years? (Oct 2026); The Real Deal, Rivani's Miami Beach office expansion heads to voters (Jul 22, 2026)

State Amendment 1: Budget Stabilization Fund

Statewide. Needs 60% YES to pass.

🪨 YES: tribe hide more food in cave for bad storm time. Big pile, up to quarter. NO: pile stay small, same as now.

YES means: The state's rainy-day savings cap rises from 10% to 25% of general revenue; the Legislature must deposit the lesser of $750M or the amount needed to reach 25% each year (unless conditions apply), and may withdraw for critical needs.
NO means: Cap stays at 10% under current rules.

Supporters: bigger cushion for hurricanes and downturns. Opponents: locks up money that could fund services now.

Sources: Florida Division of Elections, Proposed Constitutional Amendments booklet (updated Sep 3, 2026); NBC 6, Florida amendments 2026: What voters need to know (Oct 2026); Ocala Gazette, Voter's guide to Florida's 3 proposed amendments (2026)

State Amendment 2: Tax exemption for farm equipment/property on agricultural land

Statewide. Needs 60% YES.

🪨 YES: farmer no pay tax on farm tool and stuff. NO: farmer keep pay tax on tool.

YES means: Tangible personal property (e.g., equipment) on land classified agricultural, used for farming or agritourism and owned by the landowner/leaseholder, becomes exempt from property tax starting 2027.
NO means: That property stays taxable as now.

Supporters: helps farms and food security. Opponents: special-interest break (reported cost about $31M/year).

Sources: Florida Division of Elections, Proposed Constitutional Amendments booklet (updated Sep 3, 2026); NBC 6, Florida amendments 2026: What voters need to know (Oct 2026); Ocala Gazette, Voter's guide to Florida's 3 proposed amendments (2026)

State Amendment 3: Bigger homestead exemption; lower cap on non-homestead assessments

Statewide. Needs 60% YES.

🪨 YES: home-cave owner pay much less tax. Other cave tax grow slower. Chief must still pay for fire-men and spear-men first. NO: tax rule stay same. Chief keep same rocks for town.

YES means: Homestead exemption for non-school taxes rises to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028 (then inflation-adjusted); non-homestead assessment increase cap drops from 10% to 5%; new rules for newcomers and on how local governments use property-tax revenue.
NO means: Current exemptions and the 10% cap remain.

Supporters (including Byron Donalds) say owners need relief. Opponents (including many sheriffs and David Jolly) warn of local service cuts; state estimates cited by PolitiFact/Ocala Gazette are roughly $4.9B in local losses in 2027–28 rising to ~$12B a year.

Sources: Florida Division of Elections, Proposed Constitutional Amendments booklet (updated Sep 3, 2026); NBC 6, Florida amendments 2026: What voters need to know (Oct 2026); PolitiFact, Amendment 3 and public safety funding (Oct 13, 2026); News4Jax, Donalds vs. Jolly on AI, data centers, Amendment 3 (Oct 6, 2026)

County Referendum 1: Independent Salary Commission

Countywide (Miami-Dade). Simple majority.

🪨 YES: other wise people pick how much rock county chiefs get. Chiefs no can change. NO: chief pay stay fixed like now.

YES means: A five-member independent commission (appointed by the Chief Judge, State Attorney, Public Defender, Ethics Commission director and Inspector General) sets County Commissioner pay each year, and the Commission cannot change it.
NO means: Commissioner salary stays the fixed amount in the Charter.

Sources: Miami-Dade Elections, General Election Master Ballot (FINAL), Nov 3, 2026; Miami-Dade Elections, Official Sample Ballot, General Election Nov 3, 2026

County Referendum 2: Longer district residency to run for Commission

Countywide (Miami-Dade). Simple majority.

🪨 YES: must live in cave-place one whole year before run for chief. NO: half year still enough.

YES means: Candidates must live in their district at least 1 year (up from 6 months) before qualifying.
NO means: Stays 6 months.

Sources: Miami-Dade Elections, General Election Master Ballot (FINAL), Nov 3, 2026; Miami-Dade Elections, Official Sample Ballot, General Election Nov 3, 2026

County Referendum 3: Sync vacancy elections; municipal qualifying standards

Countywide (Miami-Dade). Simple majority.

🪨 YES: when chief leave to chase other job, pick new chief on same vote-day as other votes. Save effort. NO: rule stay same.

YES means: County can schedule special elections for vacated Mayor/Commission seats (when someone resigns to run for another office) together with other elections; sets minimum standards for city candidate qualifying periods.
NO means: Current timing rules stay.

Sources: Miami-Dade Elections, General Election Master Ballot (FINAL), Nov 3, 2026; Miami-Dade Elections, Official Sample Ballot, General Election Nov 3, 2026

County Referendum 4: Charter changes for elected Sheriff, Tax Collector, Property Appraiser, Elections Supervisor

Countywide (Miami-Dade). Simple majority.

🪨 YES: fix rule-rock to match new chosen chiefs (sheriff, tax man, land counter, vote keeper). Worker who run must take break. NO: rule-rock not fixed.

YES means: Updates the Charter for these now-elected offices, clarifies Clerk vs. County finance/budget duties, and requires county employees to take leave when running for those offices.
NO means: Charter text is not updated this way.

Sources: Miami-Dade Elections, General Election Master Ballot (FINAL), Nov 3, 2026; Miami-Dade Elections, Official Sample Ballot, General Election Nov 3, 2026

County Referendum 5: Technical changes

Countywide (Miami-Dade). Simple majority.

🪨 YES: clean up words on rule-rock. NO: leave old words.

YES means: Updates place names, allows extra public-notice/participation methods when state law allows, and cleans up grammar/numbering.
NO means: Charter wording stays as is.

Sources: Miami-Dade Elections, General Election Master Ballot (FINAL), Nov 3, 2026; Miami-Dade Elections, Official Sample Ballot, General Election Nov 3, 2026

County Referendum 6: More advertising in parks

Countywide (Miami-Dade). Simple majority.

🪨 YES: more picture-sign in park, rock from sign go to park. NO: only sign for sponsor, like now.

YES means: Ads in certain county parks no longer need to be tied to event/facility sponsorship, if they don't unreasonably impair park use or damage the park; money goes only to parks.
NO means: Only sponsorship-related ads allowed, as now.

Sources: Miami-Dade Elections, General Election Master Ballot (FINAL), Nov 3, 2026; Miami-Dade Elections, Official Sample Ballot, General Election Nov 3, 2026

School Board Referendum: Renew 1-mill property tax for teacher pay and school safety

Countywide (Miami-Dade). Simple majority.

🪨 YES: keep same small school tax 4 more winter. Pay teacher-shaman, keep young ones safe. NO: tax end, less rock for school.

YES means: Keeps the existing 1-mill school operating levy from July 1, 2027 to June 30, 2031 for teacher/instructional pay and school security (incl. charter schools), with citizen oversight.
NO means: The levy is not renewed after it expires.

Sources: Miami-Dade Elections, General Election Master Ballot (FINAL), Nov 3, 2026; Miami-Dade Elections, Official Sample Ballot, General Election Nov 3, 2026
